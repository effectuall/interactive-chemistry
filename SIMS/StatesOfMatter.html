Solid State

Particles are tightly packed in a regular, repeating pattern called a crystal lattice. Strong intermolecular forces keep them in place.

  • Motion: Only vibration in fixed spots.
  • Shape: Definite.
  • Volume: Definite.

Liquid State

Particles are close together but have enough kinetic energy to overcome some attractive forces, allowing them to flow around each other.

  • Motion: Random, sliding motion.
  • Shape: Takes shape of container.
  • Volume: Definite.

Gaseous State

Particles are far apart and move independently. Intermolecular forces are negligible compared to their kinetic energy.

  • Motion: High-speed, random straight lines.
  • Shape: Indefinite.
  • Volume: Indefinite.

Plasma State

An "ionized gas" where electrons are stripped from nuclei. Highly sensitive to electromagnetic fields.

  • Properties: Conducts electricity.
  • Visible: Stars, lightning, neon light.
